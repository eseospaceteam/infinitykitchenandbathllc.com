Accessible Remodeling — Avondale, AZ

Accessible Home Remodeling in Avondale, AZ

Bathrooms and homes that keep working as the people in them change — planned at your Avondale home, with a free in-home consultation.

Most remodeling is about how a room looks. This is about whether someone can still use it. In Avondale that is rarely a retirement question — it is a young city, with a median age of about 33 in the Census Bureau's 2020–2024 American Community Survey. The reasons here are usually different: making room for a parent, planning for three generations under one roof, recovering from an injury or surgery, living with a disability, or staying put in a house you have owned for years.

It covers a range. At one end, a tub swapped for a low-threshold shower so nobody has to lift a leg over a wall to wash. At the other, a whole house reworked around a wheelchair. Most projects sit in between, and most start because something has changed — a fall, a diagnosis, a parent moving in, or simply the realisation that the bathroom is going to become a problem before the house does.

We are not a walk-in tub company with a scripted pitch. We are a design-build remodeler that does this work properly, which mostly means telling you which changes are worth making and which are being sold to you.

The four services

Not sure which applies? Curbless and zero-entry showers is the usual starting point, and the accessibility guides go through the decisions in detail.

What the ADA standard does and does not require of your home

This is the single most misrepresented thing in accessible remodeling, so it is worth being blunt about. The ADA Standards for Accessible Design govern public accommodations — businesses, government buildings, places open to the public. They do not apply to a private home. No homeowner is obliged to meet them, and any contractor implying you must be brought into compliance is selling you something.

What the standard is genuinely useful for is as a set of tested dimensions. It is the most carefully researched answer available to "how much room does a wheelchair actually need", and we design to it because it works, not because anyone is compelled to. The figures we use are a 60-inch turning space (§304.3.1), a 32-inch clear width at a doorway (§404.2.3), and grab bars mounted 33 to 36 inches above the floor (§609.4), all verified against ADA.gov and the U.S. Access Board.

For most households the honest answer is that you want some of it. A curbless entry and blocking in the walls, yes. Widening every doorway in the house, usually not. We will tell you which is which for your home rather than quoting the whole standard at you.

The detail that has to happen before the walls close

Blocking is the one thing that cannot be added later. It is the timber fixed between the studs behind the finished wall, and it is what a grab bar anchors into. Fitted during the remodel it costs almost nothing; retrofitted afterwards it means opening a finished wall, and a bar screwed into tile and drywall alone will not hold a fall.

So we install blocking around the shower, beside the toilet and along the tub wall on accessible projects as a matter of course, whether or not a bar is going on it today. If the need arrives in ten years, the wall is already ready. This is the stage in our process where the difference gets made — see how a remodel runs.

Accessible remodeling in Avondale: permits, HOAs and the house you have

Most Avondale homes are newer than people expect. The median house in the city was built in 2002, and most neighborhoods are tract subdivisions from the 1980s through the 2000s. That usually helps: the framing is standard, and a tub-shower combination in a hall bath of that era tends to convert cleanly to a low-threshold shower. The city's oldest neighborhoods — Old Town, Cashion and Las Ligas — are a different job (Cashion's median house was built around 1965), with doorways and plumbing worth checking before a layout is set.

Permits. Residential permits come from the City of Avondale's Building Services Division (Development Services Department, 11465 W. Civic Center Dr., (623) 333-4000), filed online through Accela. The city's code exempts finish work such as tiling, cabinets and countertops, but plumbing, electrical, gas and HVAC changes are not exempt, and framing changes like a widened doorway are not on the exempt list either — we confirm the scope with Building Services before work starts. A rebuilt shower is inspected at the pre-slope and again at the shower liner test, before any tile goes on. We pull the City of Avondale permits for our work and book the inspections.

HOAs. Exterior changes — a ramp, a wider entry, a new walkway — change how the front of the house looks, and the city does not track HOA covenants. It tells owners to check with their HOA before applying for a permit. If your street has an HOA, we put its review into the schedule at the start rather than finding out halfway through.

Water and heat. Avondale has very hard water — about 21 grains per gallon for city well water in the city's 2022 water quality report — so handheld showers, thermostatic valves and linear drains get chosen for how easily they descale. Outdoors, National Weather Service 1991–2020 normals for the area put the July average high around 106°F, with about 111 days a year at 100°F or more. Ramp surfaces, handrails and thresholds that sit in full sun need materials that stay usable, and a zero-step entry needs drainage that copes with a monsoon storm (the season officially runs June 15 to September 30).

Paying for it

Accessible work is one of the few remodeling categories with funding attached to it. If the homeowner is a veteran, the VA administers home adaptation grants that can apply to exactly this kind of bathroom work — we have set out how that process generally runs on VA bathroom remodeling grants.

For owner-occupied homes on City of Avondale water, the city's Home Repair Program (Neighborhood & Family Services, (623) 333-2703) lists ADA work — wider doors, grab bars, ramps — among the repairs it can help with. Eligibility rules apply, so ask the city what currently qualifies before you plan around it.

Beyond that, the ordinary routes apply: see financing, and what a walk-in shower costs or what a tub-to-shower conversion costs for the ranges before anyone has seen your bathroom.
