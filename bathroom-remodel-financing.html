Bathroom Remodel Financing Options in Avondale, AZ

By Steve Hunt, Owner & Lead Designer at Infinity Kitchens and Baths

Updated November 20, 2026 • 10 min read

Reviewed by Steve Hunt, Owner & Lead Designer · AZ ROC #339999 · Last reviewed November 20, 2026

Quick answer: Arizona homeowners usually pay for a bathroom remodel with a home equity loan or HELOC (lowest rates, but your home is the collateral), a personal loan (fast and unsecured, higher rates), a cash-out refinance, an FHA 203(k) or Title I loan, or contractor-arranged financing. Through third-party lending partners, Infinity Kitchens and Baths can connect qualified clients with fixed-rate installment loans from $5,000 to $100,000.

A bathroom remodel is a significant investment, and most homeowners don't pay entirely out of pocket. The good news: Arizona homeowners have several solid financing options. Here's a clear comparison so you can choose what fits your situation.

Note: Interest rates, terms, and approval depend on your credit and current market conditions. Always compare offers and talk to a lender or financial advisor before deciding.

Home Equity Loan or HELOC

If you have equity in your home, these usually carry the lowest rates because the home secures the debt. The Federal Trade Commission describes the difference simply: a home equity loan is a lump sum, usually with a fixed APR and equal monthly payments over a set term, while a HELOC is "a revolving line of credit, similar to a credit card, except it's secured by your home," typically with a variable APR, a draw period and a repayment period. The trade-off for both: your home is the collateral, and there are closing costs.

A HELOC suits a remodel done in phases, because you draw only what you need. A home equity loan suits a single bathroom project with a fixed price, because the payment never changes.

You get three days to change your mind. When your main home secures the loan, federal law lets you cancel within three business days, for any reason and without penalty. For a home equity loan, the FTC says the clock starts on the first business day after you sign the closing documents and receive both the Truth in Lending disclosure and two copies of the cancellation notice. The right does not apply to a vacation or second home.

Interest and taxes. IRS Publication 936 (for 2025 returns) says interest on a home equity loan or line is deductible only if the money is used to buy, build or substantially improve the home that secures the loan. Whether a bathroom remodel qualifies, and whether itemizing helps you, is a question for your tax preparer.

Cash-Out Refinance

A cash-out refinance replaces your existing mortgage with a larger one and gives you the difference in cash. It can make sense if you can also improve your rate. If your current mortgage rate is lower than today's rates, though, refinancing the whole balance just to fund a remodel may cost more over time than a separate loan. Run the numbers with your lender.

Personal Loans

Unsecured personal loans fund quickly and don't put your home at risk, but they carry higher interest rates than home-equity options. They're a reasonable fit for smaller or mid-size projects when you don't want to tap home equity, or when you've owned your home only a few years. Credit unions often offer competitive home improvement loans for members, so check yours before you accept the first offer.

FHA 203(k) and Title I Loans

Two federal programs back remodel financing through FHA-approved lenders. Both come with more paperwork than a HELOC, so they make the most sense when you lack equity or are buying the home.

FHA 203(k): Limited and Standard

Limited 203(k): HUD says it lets homebuyers and homeowners finance up to $75,000 into their mortgage for minor remodeling and non-structural repairs. A 203(k) consultant is optional. HUD raised the cap from $35,000 effective November 4, 2024.

Standard 203(k): for major renovations and structural work. The repairs must cost at least $5,000, and the lender brings in a HUD-approved 203(k) consultant.

FHA Title I property improvement loan

Federal rules cap a single-family property improvement loan at $25,000, with terms of up to 20 years. The FDIC's guide to the program says a loan over $7,500 must be secured by a recorded lien on the home, and only lenders approved by HUD for Title I can make these loans, so you apply through one of them, not through HUD.

City of Avondale Programs Worth Checking

Neither of these pays for a cosmetic remodel, so treat them as a supplement to your financing, not a replacement. Both are run by the city, which sets the rules — call to confirm eligibility before you plan around them.

  • Home Repair Program. Run by the city’s Neighborhood & Family Services department, (623) 333-2703. It’s for owner-occupied homes that receive City of Avondale water service, and it covers emergency or substantial repairs — plumbing leaks, water heaters, roofs, heating and cooling, electrical — plus accessibility work such as grab bars, wider doors, ramps, and accessible showers or tubs. Emergency repairs are grants. If a failing shower or a safety need is what’s driving your project, ask the city about this program first.
  • Low Flow Plumbing Rebate. City water customers who replace higher-use fixtures can get $75 per toilet (1.6 gallons per flush or less) and $25 per showerhead (under 2.6 gpm). Apply within one year with a dated receipt; it’s first come, first served. The city also offers a free low-flow kit — call (623) 333-4400.

Veterans have one more route worth checking before borrowing: the VA's HISA, SAH and SHA grants can pay for accessibility work, including bathrooms. Our guide to VA bathroom remodel grants covers who qualifies.

A Word on Credit Cards

Credit cards can cover small line items or a 0% intro-APR period, but their standard rates are high. They're not a good fit for financing an entire remodel unless you can pay the balance before the promotional period ends.

How Much Should You Finance for a Bathroom Remodel?

Borrow against a real number, not a guess. On our own projects, the ranges on our bathroom remodel cost guide are:

  • Cosmetic update, $5,000 to $12,000: new vanity, toilet, mirror, fixtures and fresh paint.
  • Mid-range remodel, $15,000 to $35,000: new tile shower, vanity, flooring, lighting and updated plumbing fixtures.
  • Full master bath renovation, $40,000 to $80,000 and up: layout changes, custom walk-in shower, soaking tub, dual vanity, heated floor and full tile work.

Is $5,000 or $10,000 enough to remodel a bathroom? Either amount fits the cosmetic tier, with $5,000 at the very bottom of it. Once a new tile shower is part of the plan, you're in the mid-range tier. Our remodel cost calculator gives a quick ballpark before the free in-home visit, where we write the detailed estimate a lender will ask for.

Before You Sign: A Bathroom Financing Checklist

  • Get the written estimate first. Apply for the amount the project actually costs, so you don't borrow too little or pay interest on money you don't need.
  • Compare APRs, not monthly payments. A lower payment over a longer term can cost more in total. Under federal Truth in Lending rules, an ad that quotes a payment amount or a number of payments must also state the APR, so look for it.
  • Ask whether the rate is fixed or variable. HELOCs are usually variable; home equity loans and our partners' installment loans are fixed.
  • Know your cancellation window. Loans secured by your main home come with a three-business-day right to cancel.
  • Check the contractor, too. Look up the license on the Arizona Registrar of Contractors site before you sign anything. Ours is AZ ROC #339999.
  • Ask about city help. If a failing shower or a safety need is driving the job, call about the City of Avondale programs above before you borrow.

How to Choose

For a large remodel with available equity, a home equity loan or HELOC usually offers the best rate. For speed, or if you lack equity, contractor-arranged financing or a personal loan can be the better fit. If the project is about safety, such as grab bars or a lower shower entry, plan it with our aging-in-place remodeling team and ask the city and the VA about help first, since a grant can shrink the loan. We're happy to talk through the options and provide the detailed written estimate any lender will want to see.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can you finance a bathroom remodel?

Yes. Through third-party lending partners, Infinity can connect qualified clients with fixed-rate installment loans from $5,000 to $100,000, and homeowners also commonly use home equity loans, a HELOC, personal loans, or cash-out refinancing. Ask us about current options at your consultation.

What is the best way to finance a bathroom remodel?

If you have equity and a fixed project price, a home equity loan usually gives the lowest fixed rate; a HELOC is better for a remodel done in phases. Without equity, or if you want to start quickly, a personal loan or a lender partner arranged through your contractor is simpler. Compare the APR on every offer.

What's the cheapest way to finance a bathroom remodel?

If you have home equity, a home equity loan or HELOC usually offers the lowest interest rate because it's secured by your home. The trade-offs are closing costs and using your home as collateral.

Is contractor financing a good option?

It can be. Applying through your contractor is convenient, decisions can come the same day, and you don't wait on a bank appraisal. Our partners' loans are fixed-rate installment loans with the lender, not in-house credit. Compare the APR against home-equity and credit union offers to see what fits.

Is $10,000 enough to renovate a bathroom?

For a cosmetic update, usually yes: our cosmetic tier runs $5,000 to $12,000 for a new vanity, toilet, mirror, fixtures and paint. A remodel with a new tile shower typically falls in our $15,000 to $35,000 mid-range tier.

Can I cancel a home equity loan after I sign?

If the loan is secured by your main home, yes: federal law gives you three business days to cancel for any reason, without penalty. The right does not apply to a second or vacation home.

Should I use a credit card for my remodel?

Generally only for small line items or a 0% intro-APR window you can pay off in time. Standard credit-card rates are high, making them a poor fit for financing an entire remodel.

Does the City of Avondale help pay for bathroom work?

Not for cosmetic remodels, but two city programs can cover part of some projects. The Home Repair Program helps owner-occupied homes on City of Avondale water service with emergency or substantial repairs, such as plumbing leaks and water heaters, plus accessibility work like grab bars and accessible showers or tubs; emergency repairs are grants. The Low Flow Plumbing Rebate pays City water customers $75 per toilet (1.6 gallons per flush or less) and $25 per showerhead (under 2.6 gpm). Call the city to confirm eligibility before you plan around either one.
