How It Works

Our Bathroom Remodeling Process, Start to Finish

Every Infinity Kitchens and Baths project follows a proven six-step process designed to keep you informed, on schedule, and fully satisfied at every stage.

01

Free In-Home Consultation & Design

Your bathroom remodel begins with a free, no-obligation consultation at your home. One of our designers visits your space, takes detailed measurements, and has an honest conversation about your goals — what you want to accomplish, what's frustrating you about the current bathroom, and what features matter most. This isn't a sales call. It's a design conversation, and there's never any pressure to sign on the same day.

From that discussion, our design team creates a computer-aided 3D rendering of your new bathroom. You'll see the tile choices, vanity placement, shower layout, and fixture selections rendered in detail before we touch a single wall. This step eliminates surprises, prevents costly mid-project changes, and gives you the confidence to move forward knowing exactly what you're getting. Most consultations take 60–90 minutes and are completely free of charge.

02

Material Selection & Factory-Direct Ordering

Once the design is approved, we move into material selection. Because Infinity Kitchens and Baths purchases materials factory-direct, you'll have access to tile, shower systems, vanities, countertops, fixtures, and flooring at prices that are often significantly below what you'd find at retail home improvement stores. This isn't a standard trade discount — it's a structural advantage we've built into our business model since we opened in 2011.

Our team guides you through every selection with real-world context: which tile choices hold up to daily use, which shower systems are easiest to maintain, which countertop surfaces make sense for your bathroom's traffic level and cleaning habits. Every material is ordered, verified, and staged before your project starts — no mid-project delays waiting on backorders that push your timeline back weeks.

03

Demolition & Subfloor Preparation

Professional demolition is more careful than it looks. Our crew protects your home throughout the demo phase — laying floor protection through all traffic areas, sealing doorways to contain dust, and carefully disconnecting plumbing and electrical before removing fixtures. We handle haul-away of all demolition debris as part of the project scope.

Whether your home is one of Avondale's 1990s–2000s tract houses or an older home near Old Town, we always inspect behind walls and under floors for moisture damage, failed waterproofing, dry rot, or mold before proceeding. If we find a problem, we address it immediately and communicate the findings to you transparently. Our policy is zero surprises buried in a change order later — any additional scope gets discussed and approved before work continues. Rough plumbing and electrical work, including any repositioning of supply lines, drain lines, or circuits required by the new layout, is handled at this stage.

04

Waterproofing & Substrate Installation

The most important step in any bathroom remodel is one most homeowners never see: the waterproofing. It's what separates a bathroom that lasts 30 years from one that develops mold problems within five. We use industry-standard waterproofing membranes on all shower pans, shower walls, and wet areas — and we slope every shower floor correctly toward the drain at the required 1/4-inch-per-foot pitch.

For tile installations, we use cement backer board as the substrate on all wet surfaces, properly fastened and taped at every seam. For groutless solid-surface shower systems, the waterproofing is built directly into the panel system itself — which is one of the key reasons those systems carry a lifetime warranty. We test all waterproofing and drain connections before applying any finish surface — and on permitted shower work, the City of Avondale's inspection list includes a pre-slope and shower liner test before tile goes on. This step adds time to the project, but skipping it is how contractors create problems that homeowners discover years later when it's much more expensive to fix.

05

Tile, Surfaces & Fixture Installation

This is the stage where your new bathroom comes to life. Every tile installation begins with a detailed layout drawing that accounts for the tile pattern, grout joint spacing, cut placement, and edge treatments — nothing is eyeballed. Our tile setters are experienced with everything from large-format 24×48 porcelain slabs to intricate mosaic patterns and natural stone that requires sealing before grouting.

Vanities are leveled, secured to wall studs, and fitted with countertops cut to exact dimensions. Plumbing fixtures — faucets, showerheads, valves, and drain trim — are installed to manufacturer specifications and pressure-tested before walls are closed. Frameless glass shower enclosures are measured on-site and installed with proper tolerances and hardware. Lighting, exhaust ventilation, and accessories are handled as part of the finish phase, so everything is coordinated and complete before we call the project done. With very hard water, we also steer finish choices toward surfaces that hide mineral spotting and are easy to wipe down.

06

Final Walkthrough, Punch List & Warranty

Before we consider a project complete, we conduct a thorough walkthrough with you present. We examine every tile edge, every caulk joint, every fixture connection, every door and drawer, every threshold transition. Any item that doesn't meet our standards — or yours — goes on a punch list and gets addressed before we collect final payment. This walkthrough typically takes 30–45 minutes, and our team takes it seriously.

Once complete, we review the care and maintenance guidelines for your specific materials: which cleaners to use on your tile, how to care for natural stone, how to maintain grout long-term, and what to do if you ever need warranty service. Our workmanship is warranted in writing, and many of our products carry manufacturer warranties ranging from several years to full lifetime coverage. We want you to be a customer for life — and the only way that happens is if we hold ourselves to this standard on every project, every time.

Permits & Inspections

Who issues a bathroom remodel permit in Avondale

Avondale handles its own permits. Plan review, permit issuance and inspections sit with the City of Avondale's Building Services Division, part of the Development Services Department at 11465 W. Civic Center Dr. (623-333-4000), and applications are filed online through the city's Accela portal.

What needs a permit depends on scope. The city's code exempts finish work — painting, tiling, carpeting, cabinets and countertops — from a building permit on its own. Plumbing, electrical, gas and mechanical work does need one: replacing or moving valves, pipes or fixtures, relocating a drain, or adding a circuit for lighting, an exhaust fan or a heated floor. Most real bathroom remodels include at least some of that.

Showers get particular attention. The city's residential inspection list includes a pre-slope and shower liner test, so the pan is checked for slope and leaks before any tile covers it. Converting a tub to a walk-in shower, moving a drain or taking out a curb all change waterproofing, floor build-up and often framing — exactly the work an inspector looks at.

We pull the permit and meet the inspector ourselves. You should not be the one learning a municipal submittal process in the middle of your own remodel — and a contractor who suggests skipping the permit on a job that needs one is telling you something useful about how they work. One thing to do first: if your home is in an HOA, check its rules. The city does not track HOA covenants and tells owners to confirm with their HOA before applying.

City of Avondale Building Services publishes the current requirements. Our Avondale permit guide walks through the process in more detail.

Local Housing Stock

The Avondale bathrooms we work in

Avondale is a young city in housing terms. Census figures put the median home's construction year at 2002, and about six in ten homes were built in the 1990s or 2000s. The older parts of town — Old Town along Western Ave, Cashion and Las Ligas — are a small share of the total.

So most bathrooms we open are original builder-grade rooms: a fiberglass or acrylic tub/shower combination, a basic vanity, a small exhaust fan, and fixtures picked for price rather than for the water they would face. Behind the walls those homes are usually sound, and the work is genuinely a remodel rather than a repair. An older house is a different job — supply lines, venting and circuits can all need attention once the wall is open. We price those two situations very differently, and we tell you which one you have before you sign anything.

It is a family-heavy city, too — the median resident is in their early thirties — so the hall bath that kids share often matters as much as the primary suite. Durable, easy-clean surfaces earn their place there.

Very hard water

Avondale's tap water is a blend of city well water and Colorado River water, and the city's 2022 water quality report put city well water at about 21 grains per gallon — "very hard" on the report's own scale. You see it as white spotting on shower glass, haze in grout lines, and scale on showerheads and faucet aerators.

That changes what is worth specifying: groutless solid-surface walls or large-format tile with fewer joints, treated or lightly textured glass, brushed rather than polished metal finishes, and quality valves and cartridges that tolerate mineral build-up. A whole-house water softener helps every fixture in the house, and we will plan the bathroom around one if you have it or are adding it. Our guide to faucets and fixtures for hard water goes further.

Heat, monsoon dust and ventilation

Avondale sits in the low desert at about 1,000 feet. National Weather Service 1991–2020 normals for the area put the July average high around 106°F, with roughly 111 days a year at 100°F or hotter, and monsoon season runs June 15 to September 30. Bathrooms dry quickly most of the year, but monsoon humidity and dust are real, and a builder-grade fan is often too small or poorly ducted. We size and duct the exhaust fan properly on every remodel — see our ventilation and moisture guide.

City water rebates

The City of Avondale's Low Flow Plumbing Rebate pays $75 per toilet (1.6 gallons per flush or less) and $25 per showerhead (under 2.6 gallons per minute) for City water customers replacing higher-use fixtures. Apply within a year with a dated receipt; funds are first come, first served. Check the city's rebate programs page for current terms — we will give you the fixture specs and receipts you need to apply.

Common Questions

Bathroom Remodeling FAQs

How long does a bathroom remodel take in Avondale, AZ?

Most of our bathroom remodels take between 2 and 5 weeks from demolition to completion. A simple tub-to-shower conversion or vanity update can often be completed in 5–7 business days. A full gut-and-rebuild of a master bathroom — custom tile shower, frameless glass, double vanity, new flooring — typically runs 3–5 weeks. Lead times vary based on material availability and overall project complexity. We provide a detailed written timeline at your consultation and give you regular progress updates so you're never left wondering where things stand.

Do I need a permit for a bathroom remodel in Avondale?

It depends on the scope. The City of Avondale's Building Services Division issues and inspects residential permits. Finish work on its own — tile, cabinets, countertops, paint — needs no building permit, but replacing or moving plumbing valves, pipes or fixtures, relocating a drain, or adding electrical circuits does, and shower work is inspected with a pre-slope and shower liner test. Infinity Kitchens and Baths pulls the permit and meets the inspector as part of the job, so this is our paperwork rather than yours. If you are in an HOA, check its rules first — the city does not track them.

What is a groutless shower system, and is it worth the extra cost?

A groutless shower system uses solid-surface acrylic or composite panels — instead of tile — to create the shower walls and sometimes the floor. Because the surface is completely non-porous and seamless, mold and mildew can't penetrate it, and there's no grout to clean, reseal, or replace over time. These systems typically cost somewhat more to install than a standard tile shower, but the maintenance savings are substantial. No annual grout sealing, no mold remediation, no tile replacement — and with Avondale's very hard water, no grout lines to stain. We back our groutless installations with a full lifetime warranty from both Infinity Kitchens and Baths and the manufacturer. The majority of customers who choose groutless say it's the best decision they made in the remodel.

How much does a bathroom remodel cost in Avondale, AZ?

Costs vary significantly based on scope, material selections, and whether plumbing or electrical needs to be relocated. In general, a partial update — new shower walls, new vanity, new flooring — typically runs $8,000–$15,000. A full master bathroom remodel with a custom tile shower, frameless glass enclosure, double vanity, and new flooring typically runs $18,000–$35,000 or more depending on material choices. We provide detailed written estimates after your free in-home consultation — no surprises, no pressure. We also offer financing options for qualifying projects, which allows many homeowners to do the full remodel they want rather than making compromises on scope or quality.

Can you remodel a small bathroom or half-bath?

Absolutely — small bathrooms are actually where smart design makes the biggest difference. Strategic layout adjustments, light-colored tile, frameless glass shower enclosures, floating vanities, and well-planned storage solutions can transform a cramped bathroom into a space that feels significantly larger and more functional than its square footage would suggest. We've renovated everything from tiny guest bathrooms to narrow half-baths and kids' hall baths. During your free consultation, we'll bring specific design ideas tailored to your exact space and goals.

Yes. Infinity Kitchens and Baths holds Arizona ROC License #339999 and is fully licensed, bonded, and insured for all remodeling work throughout the state. Every crew member is background-checked and drug-tested. We carry general liability insurance and workers' compensation coverage on every job site. You should always verify contractor licensing before hiring anyone in Arizona — you can check any contractor's current status at the Arizona Registrar of Contractors website at roc.az.gov. Never hire a contractor who can't provide you with a current, active ROC license number before work begins.

How old are most Avondale homes, and does that change the job?

Most are not old. Census figures put the median Avondale home's construction year at 2002, and about six in ten homes were built in the 1990s or 2000s, so the typical bathroom we open is an original builder-grade room that is sound behind the walls. Older homes around Old Town and Cashion can need supply lines, venting or circuits updated once the wall is open. We confirm what is behind your walls before quoting rather than after demolition.

How does Avondale's hard water affect a bathroom remodel?

The city's 2022 water quality report put city well water at about 21 grains per gallon, which the report itself calls "very hard." It shows up as spotting on glass, stained grout and scale on fixtures. We usually recommend groutless walls or large-format tile with fewer joints, treated glass, brushed finishes and fixtures that tolerate mineral build-up — and a water softener if you are open to one.

Does the City of Avondale offer rebates for low-flow toilets and showerheads?

Yes. The city's Low Flow Plumbing Rebate pays $75 per toilet (1.6 gallons per flush or less) and $25 per showerhead (under 2.6 gallons per minute) for City water customers replacing higher-use fixtures. You apply within a year with a dated receipt, and funds are first come, first served, so check the city's rebate page for current terms. We will give you the fixture specs and receipts you need.
