Bathroom Vanities for Avondale Homes

The Vanity Sets the Tone for Your Entire Bathroom

Walk into almost any bathroom and the first thing your eye lands on is the vanity. It anchors the room visually, provides the storage you rely on every single morning, and frames the mirror that starts and ends your day. A poorly chosen or worn-out vanity makes even an otherwise attractive bathroom feel dated and cramped. A beautifully crafted, well-fitted vanity transforms the whole space — and that transformation is exactly what Infinity Kitchens and Baths delivers for homeowners in Avondale. With the median Avondale home built in 2002, the vanity we replace is often the original builder-grade unit, and after two decades of daily use and very hard water it usually shows it.

Quality matters enormously when it comes to bathroom vanities. The cabinets you choose should be built with solid wood or plywood box construction, not particleboard that swells and crumbles when moisture inevitably finds its way in. Dovetail drawer joints, soft-close hinges and drawer glides, and proper plumbing rough-in clearances are the details that separate a vanity that lasts 30 years from one that starts showing its age in five. We specify these construction standards on every project, and we walk you through what to look for so you can make a confident, informed decision. Our full bathroom remodeling approach covers every trade — from demo through final hardware — under one coordinated team.

One of the biggest advantages of working with Infinity is our factory-direct relationships with cabinet suppliers. Most retail showrooms add two or three layers of markup before a product reaches your home. Because we buy direct and move real volume, we pass those savings back to you — meaning you can often afford a significantly better vanity for the same budget you had in mind when you first started shopping. Share a wish-list photo at your free in-home consultation and let us show you what’s achievable. Bathroom Vanity Styles We Install

Every bathroom is different — different footprint, different plumbing layout, different design vision. We install the full range of vanity configurations so your solution fits your space perfectly, not just approximately.

Single Vanity (36–60″)

The most common configuration for guest bathrooms and primary baths with one user in mind. We size single vanities from 36 to 60 inches wide, with a range of door and drawer configurations designed to maximize usable storage in whatever footprint your bathroom offers.

Double / His & Hers Vanity

A dual-sink vanity running 60 to 72 inches or wider is the number-one upgrade request in primary bathroom remodels. Two separate sink bowls, dedicated drawer stacks, and a shared countertop running the full wall creates a spa-level primary bathroom where two people can get ready at once. Check the wall length first: the clearance table below shows why 60 inches is the tightest a double vanity can go.

Floating / Wall-Mounted Vanity

Wall-mounted vanities are suspended off the floor, creating a clean, modern profile and making floor cleaning effortless. They also make small bathrooms feel larger by keeping the floor plane visible. Installation requires proper wall blocking or a reinforced substrate, which we handle in-house during the rough-in phase.

Freestanding Furniture Vanity

Furniture-style vanities have exposed legs and a softer, more traditional aesthetic — perfect for farmhouse, transitional, and vintage-inspired bathroom designs. They pair beautifully with vessel sinks and bridge faucets. We source furniture vanities in a wide range of wood tones and painted finishes.

Corner Vanity (Small Bathrooms)

When every square inch counts — powder rooms, studio apartment baths, and compact hall baths — a corner vanity uses space that would otherwise go to waste. Angled and L-shaped configurations let us fit a functional sink and storage area without crowding the room.

Custom Built-In Vanity

When no stock or semi-custom product fits the vision — an angled wall, an unusually tall ceiling, a recessed niche, or a specific material combination — we design and build a fully custom vanity from the ground up. These projects are built to the exact millimeter of your space and finished to match your overall bathroom design perfectly.

Vanity Countertop Materials

The countertop you choose affects how your vanity looks, how it performs under daily moisture and cosmetics, and how much maintenance you’ll be doing for years to come. In Avondale it also has to live with very hard water: the city’s 2022 water quality report put city well water at about 21 grains per gallon — “very hard” on the report’s own scale — and that leaves mineral rings around faucets and drains. We offer every major material and help you weigh the trade-offs honestly.

Quartz Vanity Tops

Engineered quartz is the most popular countertop material in bathroom vanities today, and for good reason. It is non-porous, which means no annual sealing and no concern about bacteria harboring in microscopic surface pores — a real advantage in a bathroom environment. Quartz holds up to acidic toiletry products, resists staining from makeup and hair dye, and is available in more than 100 colors and patterns, including convincing marble-look options that give you the luxurious aesthetic without the upkeep. Our quartz vanity tops are templated and fabricated specifically for your cabinet run, so overhangs, sink cutouts, and backsplash returns are all precise.

Marble Vanity Tops

Marble is the gold standard of bathroom luxury. The veining patterns are entirely natural — no two slabs are identical — and the depth of color in a polished Carrara or Calacatta slab is something engineered stone simply cannot fully replicate. Marble does require annual sealing and a bit more care to avoid etching from acidic products — and with hard water, from the mineral cleaners people reach for to remove spotting — but for homeowners who want a truly elevated primary bathroom, the investment is worth it. We arrange for you to see the actual slab before fabrication so you know exactly what your finished countertop will look like.

Granite Vanity Tops

Granite offers a natural stone option that sits between quartz and marble in terms of maintenance requirements. Each slab is unique, with movement and mineral patterns that give your vanity a one-of-a-kind appearance. Properly sealed granite is highly resistant to heat, scratches, and everyday bathroom use. It comes in a broader range of colors than marble — from classic white and cream tones to dramatic blacks, blues, and earth tones — making it a versatile choice for both traditional and contemporary bathroom designs. We can arrange slab viewing so you select your specific piece.

Solid Surface / Corian

Solid surface materials like Corian and similar brands are a practical, budget-conscious option that deserves more credit than it often receives. The key advantage is the ability to produce an integrated sink — the sink bowl and countertop are molded from a single continuous piece, eliminating the seam where most grime accumulates around undermount installations. Solid surface can also be routed to virtually any shape and is repairable if scratched. For aging-in-place and accessible bathroom projects, the seamless integrated sink option is especially useful. For a deeper dive into all the countertop materials we work with, see our countertops page.

Granite Bathroom Vanities in Arizona: Granite or Quartz?

Is granite OK for a bathroom vanity? Yes. Properly sealed granite handles heat, scratches and everyday bathroom use well, and every slab is one of a kind. The catch is that it's natural stone, so it's porous and needs sealing; we recommend resealing about once a year. Quartz needs no sealing at all, which is why it's the easier choice for a busy kids' bath.

In Avondale the deciding factor is often the water. With city water averaging about 21 grains per gallon in the city's 2022 report, mineral spots show up around every faucet. Busy, speckled patterns hide those spots far better than a solid dark surface does, so it's worth choosing the slab with that in mind. We can arrange slab viewing so you pick the exact piece.

Bathroom Vanity Sizes and Clearances: Code Minimums vs. Comfort

A vanity that fits the wall can still fail the room. The plumbing code sets minimum spacing around every sink, and the National Kitchen and Bath Association (NKBA) publishes more generous planning guidelines for comfort. Avondale enforces the 2024 International Residential Code, so the code column below is what an inspector checks when plumbing is moved.

MeasurementCode minimum (IRC)NKBA recommendation
Sink centerline to a side wall or tall obstruction15 in.20 in.
Between the centerlines of two sinks30 in.36 in.
Clear floor space in front of the sink21 in.30 in.
Counter heightNo code height for homes32 to 43 in., set to the user

Worked example: will a double vanity fit? Add the numbers across the wall. At code minimums, two sinks need 15 + 30 + 15 = 60 inches. At NKBA comfort spacing they need 20 + 36 + 20 = 76 inches. So a 60-inch double vanity is legal but tight, a 72-inch unit is roomier, and on a wall shorter than 60 inches a single sink with more counter is usually the better plan. If you're planning to stay in the home long term, our aging-in-place work includes vanity height adjustments and comfort-height sinks.

How We Install Your Bathroom Vanity

Our process is deliberate and communication-forward. You’ll always know what is happening, when it’s happening, and who is responsible for it.

01

Free In-Home Measurement & Style Consultation

We start by visiting your home — not asking you to guess at measurements or browse selections blind. Our designer takes precise measurements of your bathroom, documents the existing plumbing rough-in locations, and walks through your style goals. You’ll leave the consultation with a clear picture of what is possible within your space and your budget.

02

Cabinet & Countertop Selection

Using the measurements and style notes from the consultation, we present curated cabinet options and countertop material samples. For stone countertops, we can arrange slab viewing so you choose the exact piece going on your vanity. Hardware finishes, sink types, and faucet styles are also selected at this stage so every element is coordinated before anything is ordered.

03

Plumbing Rough-In & Demo

On installation day, our crew starts by carefully removing the existing vanity, countertop, and fixtures. We inspect the supply and drain lines for any deterioration and adjust rough-in locations as needed to match the new cabinet layout. If wall blocking is required for a floating vanity, it is installed at this stage. The cabinet and countertop themselves need no building permit in Avondale, but if the job moves supply or drain lines or replaces valves and fixtures, that is plumbing work the City of Avondale permits and inspects — we pull the permit and meet the inspector. All debris is loaded out of your home before we move to the next phase.

04

Cabinet & Counter Installation

Cabinets are set, shimmed, and leveled even on uneven bathroom floors — a detail that matters for drawer alignment and door swing. Once cabinets are secured, the countertop (templated and pre-fabricated to your exact dimensions) is set and adhered. Undermount sink clips and silicone sealing are completed at this phase, along with any backsplash tile or stone if it is part of the scope.

05

Plumbing Finish, Hardware & Final Walkthrough

Faucets, drains, supply lines, and shut-off valves are all connected and tested for leaks before we call the job complete. We steer faucet choices toward ceramic-disc cartridges and finishes that hide mineral spotting — see our guide to faucets and fixtures for hard water. We install mirrors or medicine cabinets, set cabinet hardware, and do a thorough final inspection. You walk through the completed vanity with us, and we don’t consider the job done until you are completely satisfied with the result.

Bathroom Vanity FAQ

Common questions Avondale homeowners ask before getting started on a vanity project.

How long does vanity installation take?
A straightforward single-vanity swap — same footprint, no tile work, standard plumbing adjustment — typically takes one to two days from demo to finished plumbing. A double-vanity installation or one that involves wall blocking for a floating unit, new tile, or significant plumbing relocation may run two to four days. We provide a project-specific timeline at the estimate stage so you can plan accordingly.
What’s the best countertop material for a bathroom vanity?
For most homeowners, engineered quartz is the easiest long-term choice because it requires no sealing and resists nearly everything a busy bathroom throws at it. If you want natural stone and are willing to seal it annually, marble gives an unmatched luxury look and granite offers similar durability with more color variety. For a budget-friendly option with an integrated sink, solid surface is worth a serious look. We walk through all the trade-offs during your consultation so you can choose based on your real lifestyle, not just aesthetics.
Can I keep my existing plumbing with a new vanity?
In many cases, yes — if the new vanity is the same width as the existing one and the sink is in the same general position, the existing supply lines and drain can often be reused or extended with minimal work. Where it gets more complicated is when you are switching from a single sink to a double vanity, changing from a floor-mounted to a wall-mounted unit, or significantly shifting the cabinet position. We assess your existing rough-in during the in-home consultation and give you a clear picture of what plumbing work, if any, will be required.
What’s the difference between a floating and a floor-mounted vanity?
A floor-mounted (or freestanding) vanity sits on the bathroom floor with its cabinet box resting on the ground — the traditional configuration. A floating (wall-mounted) vanity is attached directly to wall studs or a reinforced substrate and hangs at a selected height with open floor space below it. Floating vanities give a cleaner, more contemporary look and make mopping the floor easier, but they do require proper structural support in the wall, which adds some labor cost. They also let you set the vanity height to suit the people using it, rather than accepting the fixed height of a floor-standing cabinet (typically 32 to 34 inches on older vanities and 34 to 36 inches on comfort-height models). NKBA planning guidelines put comfortable counter height anywhere from 32 to 43 inches, depending on the user.
Do you install mirrors and medicine cabinets too?
Yes — mirror and medicine cabinet installation is included as part of our vanity service. We can install a flat framed mirror, a frameless mirror, a single recessed medicine cabinet, or a full-width mirrored medicine cabinet depending on your preference and wall conditions. If a recessed unit is desired, we assess wall cavity depth and any obstructions before confirming feasibility. Coordinating the mirror or cabinet with your vanity and lighting is something we handle as part of the overall design process so everything looks intentional and cohesive.
Do I need a permit to replace a bathroom vanity in Avondale?
Not for the cabinet and countertop themselves — the City of Avondale’s code lists cabinets and countertops as finish work that needs no building permit. Plumbing is different: moving supply or drain lines, adding a second sink, or replacing valves and fixtures falls outside the exemption and goes through the city’s Building Services Division. We tell you at the consultation which side of that line your project falls on, and when a permit is needed we pull it and meet the inspector.
Is granite OK for a bathroom vanity?
Yes. Sealed granite resists heat, scratches and daily bathroom use, and each slab is unique. It's porous, so plan to reseal it about once a year. With Avondale's very hard water, a busy speckled pattern hides mineral spots better than a solid dark surface.
How much is a granite bathroom vanity?
Granite runs $45 to $100 per square foot installed on our projects, but a vanity top is small, so the whole vanity matters more. Installed, a stock vanity with top and sink runs about $800 to $2,500, a semi-custom vanity with a quartz top $2,000 to $5,000, and a fully custom vanity with a stone top $4,500 to $12,000 or more.
How much wall space does a double vanity need?
At least 60 inches. The residential code requires 15 inches from each sink's centerline to a side wall and 30 inches between the two centerlines (15 + 30 + 15). NKBA's comfort guidelines of 20 and 36 inches work out to 76 inches.
