The Best Time of Year to Remodel in Avondale, AZ

By Steve Hunt, Owner & Lead Designer at Infinity Kitchens and Baths

Reviewed by Steve Hunt, Owner & Lead Designer · AZ ROC #339999

Updated November 11, 2026 • 8 min read

Part of our Remodeling Process & Planning — see all 7 guides in this category.

“When should I start my remodel?” is one of the first questions homeowners ask us — and the honest answer is that a good remodeler works year-round, so there’s no season you have to avoid for interior kitchen and bathroom work. In Avondale, though, timing does affect a few real things: how hot it is when doors are open for demolition and deliveries, whether monsoon storms can reach any part of the job that’s exposed to the outdoors, how far out schedules are booked, and how long materials take to arrive. Here’s a season-by-season look at remodeling in Avondale, the city rules that shape a construction schedule, and the timing factor that matters more than the calendar.

The single most important timing tip: kitchens and bathrooms both require weeks of planning and material ordering before any demolition begins. So the best time to start the conversation is usually one to three months before you want work to begin.

Avondale’s Climate at a Glance

Avondale sits in the low desert at roughly 1,000 feet. National Weather Service 1991–2020 normals for the area put the July average high at about 106°F, with about 111 days a year at 100°F or hotter and about 21 days at 110°F or hotter. The monsoon officially runs from June 15 to September 30, and the area averages only about 7 to 8 inches of rain a year. January lows average in the 40s.

For a remodel, that means two things. Interior work goes on in every month, because it happens inside an air-conditioned house. Anything that opens the house to the outdoors — a home addition, a patio cover, a new exterior door or window, a roof penetration for a bath fan — is easiest to schedule from October through April.

Fall (October–November): The Season Opens

Fall is arguably the sweet spot for an Avondale remodel. The monsoon is over by September 30, the afternoons cool off, and finishing a kitchen or bath before the holidays is a common and very achievable goal — if you start early enough. Outdoor tasks like cutting tile and stone, hauling debris and taking deliveries become comfortable again. Fall books up fast for exactly these reasons, so start the conversation in late summer.

Winter (December–February): Mild and Underrated

Winter is the most underrated time to remodel. Demand softens after the holidays, which can mean more scheduling flexibility and a faster start, and Avondale’s winter days are mild enough for any exterior-facing part of a project. If you want the shortest wait for a start date and comfortable working conditions, winter is often it. The one thing to plan around is the holidays themselves — most families don’t want the kitchen torn out the week relatives arrive.

Spring (March–May): Popular, So Book Early

Spring is the last comfortable stretch before the triple-digit season, and many homeowners want their project wrapped up before summer. That makes it a popular window: our calendar (and every good contractor’s) fills up, so booking early is essential. If you want a spring remodel — especially one with exterior work — start planning in winter, and aim to have the outdoor-facing portion finished before the heat builds in late spring.

Summer (June–September): Heat and Monsoon

Summer is the season Avondale homeowners ask about most, and the answer is simple: interior kitchen and bathroom remodels work fine in summer. The work happens inside the house with the air conditioning running. A few things are different, and we plan for them:

  • Early starts. The City of Avondale’s permit application sets construction hours of 5 AM to 7 PM on weekdays from May 1 to October 15 (6 AM to 7 PM the rest of the year), and 7 AM to 7 PM on weekends and holidays. The earlier summer start lets crews handle deliveries, debris hauling and outdoor cutting in the cooler morning hours.
  • Keeping the house cool. Demolition and deliveries mean open doors. We keep openings short, close off the work area, and let your A/C keep the rest of the house comfortable.
  • Heat-sensitive materials. Thinset, grout, adhesives and sealants have shorter working times in heat, so we keep them inside the conditioned house, not in a garage or truck that bakes all afternoon.
  • Monsoon storms and dust. The monsoon officially runs June 15 to September 30. Dust storms and sudden downpours can affect any part of a project that’s open to the outdoors, so we sequence exterior work around the forecast and keep open walls and HVAC returns covered against dust.

Purely interior remodels are essentially unaffected by any of this. If your project includes an addition, a patio cover or new exterior openings, we’ll usually suggest scheduling that portion for October through April. And if you’d rather not compete for the busy fall and spring slots, an interior-only summer project is a practical choice.

City of Avondale Rules That Shape a Remodel Schedule

Weather isn’t the only thing on the calendar. Permits and inspections from the City of Avondale’s Building Services Division add their own rhythm to a project:

  • Some work needs no permit at all. Avondale’s building code says painting, tiling, carpeting, cabinets and countertops need no building permit, so a cabinet-and-countertop update can start as soon as materials arrive. Plumbing, electrical, gas and HVAC changes beyond minor repairs do need a permit, filed online through the city’s Accela portal.
  • Inspections run Monday through Thursday. City inspections run Monday–Thursday, 7 AM–4 PM, and requests made by 3 PM get next-business-day service. A rough-in that’s ready on a Thursday afternoon will typically wait until the following week, so we build those days into the schedule.
  • Nothing gets covered until it passes. Work has to be inspected “prior to concealment or cover.” In a bathroom, that includes a pre-slope inspection and a shower liner test before any tile goes on.
  • Permits don’t wait forever. A permit expires if no inspection is approved within 180 days — one more reason to have materials ordered before work starts.
  • Check your HOA first. If your home is in an HOA, check its rules before anything else. The city doesn’t track HOA covenants and tells owners to check with their HOA before applying for a permit — especially for anything visible from outside.

We pull City of Avondale permits for our work and schedule the inspections as part of the job. For the full rundown, see our guide to Avondale remodeling permits.

What Matters More Than the Season: Lead Time

Here’s the truth most homeowners don’t realize: the calendar season matters less than lead time. Cabinets are commonly made to order with a 2–4 week production window; custom cabinets and certain stone or tile can take longer. Add design and material selection on the front end, and a kitchen remodel typically needs several weeks of runway before demolition day. Starting the conversation early is how you get the start date you actually want — see our guide to planning a kitchen remodel and our remodel timeline.

How to Time It Right

Work backward from when you want to be finished. Want a kitchen done by Thanksgiving? Start the design conversation in late summer. Want to avoid the spring rush? Book a winter slot. Planning an addition, a patio cover or anything that opens an exterior wall? Aim the construction for October through April and start planning a few months earlier. Whenever you begin, the first step is a free in-home consultation: we come to your Avondale home, measure, talk through scope, and give you a realistic schedule. Frequently Asked Questions

When is the best time of year to remodel in Avondale?

There's no season you have to avoid for interior kitchen and bathroom work, which happens inside an air-conditioned home year-round. October through April is the most comfortable stretch in Avondale, especially for anything that touches the outside of the house, and fall and spring fill up fastest. Winter is the most underrated for its shorter wait times. The bigger factor than season is lead time — start planning one to three months before you want work to begin.

Can you remodel in Avondale during the summer?

Yes. Interior kitchen and bathroom remodels work fine in summer because the work happens inside the air-conditioned house. The City of Avondale's permit application allows construction from 5 AM on weekdays between May 1 and October 15, so crews can handle deliveries and outdoor cutting in the cooler morning hours. We keep heat-sensitive materials like thinset, grout and adhesives inside, and we schedule exterior-facing work for October through April when we can.

Does the monsoon affect remodeling in Avondale?

Only for work exposed to the outdoors. The monsoon officially runs June 15 to September 30, and dust storms and sudden downpours can affect a home addition, a patio cover, exterior changes or anything that opens the building envelope. Purely interior kitchen and bathroom remodels are essentially unaffected. We sequence exterior work around the forecast and keep open walls and HVAC returns covered against dust.

How far in advance should I book a remodel?

Plan to start the conversation one to three months before you want work to begin. Cabinets are typically made to order with a 2–4 week production lead, and design plus material selection add time on the front end. Booking early is especially important for fall and spring, which fill up fastest.

Is winter a good time to remodel in Avondale?

Yes — winter is often the best time for a fast start. Demand softens after the holidays, so scheduling is more flexible, and Avondale's winter days are mild (January lows average in the 40s), so even the exterior-facing parts of a project are comfortable to schedule.

Do City of Avondale inspections affect my schedule?

A little. City inspections run Monday through Thursday, 7 AM to 4 PM, and requests made by 3 PM get next-business-day service. Work has to pass inspection before it is covered — in a bathroom that includes a pre-slope inspection and a shower liner test before tile. A permit expires if no inspection is approved within 180 days. We schedule inspections as part of the job and build those days into your timeline. See our Avondale permit guide.
