How to Choose the Best Walk-In Shower Installer in Prescott, AZ

By Steve Hunt, Owner & Lead Designer at Infinity Kitchen & Bath

Updated June 24, 2026 • 7 min read

Above everything else, make sure they waterproof correctly — it's the make-or-break of any shower. Then weigh curbless, barrier-free design, the right shower base or pan, good glass options, and low-maintenance groutless systems, plus a detailed written estimate and a warranty. Infinity Kitchen & Bath (AZ ROC #339999) installs walk-in showers across the Prescott area and gets the waterproofing right every time.

Search "best walk-in shower installer in Prescott" and you'll see ads, directories, and a long list of companies all promising a beautiful shower. The trouble is that the most important part of a walk-in shower is the part you can't see. After installing showers across the Quad Cities, here's the honest checklist we'd use ourselves — starting with the one criterion that matters more than all the rest.

1. Waterproofing Is the Make-or-Break

This is the single most important factor, and the one most often cut to win a low bid. Behind every walk-in shower is a waterproofing system — the pan, the wall membrane, the seams and penetrations — and once tile or panels go up, it's invisible. Done right, it lasts for decades. Done wrong, water gets behind the wall and you're looking at rot, mold, and a tear-out within a few years. Before you hire anyone, ask exactly how they waterproof the pan and walls. A real installer can answer in detail and will show you.

2. Verify the License and Insurance

In Arizona, remodeling contractors must hold an active license with the Registrar of Contractors (ROC). Ask for the ROC number and look it up at roc.az.gov — it's free and takes a minute. Confirm the license is active and in the right classification, and verify general liability insurance and workers' compensation. If an installer can't or won't give you a current ROC number, walk away. (Infinity holds AZ ROC #339999.) Also favor companies that use their own in-house crews over rotating subcontractors — on waterproofing-critical work, consistent crews matter.

3. Curbless and Barrier-Free Design

A curbless, barrier-free walk-in shower removes the step-over threshold — safer for everyone and essential if accessibility is a goal. But a true curbless entry depends entirely on correct sloping and waterproofing of the pan so water drains where it should and stays inside the shower. Ask whether the installer has built curbless showers before and how they manage the slope and the linear or center drain to keep water contained.

4. Shower Base and Pan Options

You generally have two paths for the floor of the shower: a custom tile base or a solid-surface pan. A tile base offers the most design freedom but lives or dies by the waterproofing beneath it and has grout to maintain. A solid-surface pan is seamless, easy to clean, and very low-maintenance. Neither is automatically "better" — the right choice depends on your priorities, and a good installer will walk you through the trade-offs instead of pushing only what's easiest for them.

5. Glass Options: Frameless vs. Framed

Glass changes both the look and the budget. Frameless glass gives a clean, open, high-end appearance and shows off the tile or panels, but costs more. Framed and semi-frameless enclosures are more economical and still durable. In the Prescott area's hard water, ask about glass coatings that resist mineral spotting and make cleaning easier. The right installer will match the glass to your style and your budget honestly.

6. Low-Maintenance Groutless Systems, Estimates, and Warranty

If easy upkeep is a priority, ask about groutless solid-surface shower systems — they have few or no grout lines to scrub, seal, or watch for mildew, which makes them far easier to keep clean than traditional tile. Whatever system you choose, insist on a clear, itemized written estimate covering scope, materials, and timeline, and ask exactly what the workmanship warranty covers and for how long. Then look at local reviews and a real portfolio of completed showers.

Red Flags to Avoid

  • No clear answer on how they waterproof the pan and walls
  • No ROC number, or one that doesn't check out at roc.az.gov
  • Large upfront cash deposit demands
  • Pressure to decide today or "today-only" pricing
  • Vague, one-line estimates with no scope or timeline
  • No proof of insurance or workers' compensation

Questions to Ask Before You Sign

  • Exactly how do you waterproof the shower pan and walls?
  • What's your ROC license number, and are you insured?
  • Have you built curbless showers, and how do you manage the slope and drain?
  • Do you recommend a tile base or a solid-surface pan for my project, and why?
  • What glass and groutless options do you offer?
  • What does my written estimate include, and what warranty do you provide?
