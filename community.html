Our Community

Community Involvement & Local Partnerships

Serving Prescott, Prescott Valley, Chino Valley, and surrounding Yavapai County communities.

Infinity Kitchens and Baths isn't a national franchise or a regional chain. We're a small, family-owned remodeling company that was built in Prescott, for Prescott. When you work with us, you're hiring your neighbors — people who live in the same community, know the same streets, and have a stake in how your home and our shared town look and feel for years to come.

Rooted in Prescott Since 2011

Steve Hunt founded Infinity Kitchens and Baths in Prescott, Arizona in 2011. In the more than a decade since, we've completed hundreds of kitchen and bathroom remodels across Prescott, Prescott Valley, Chino Valley, Dewey-Humboldt, and the broader Yavapai County region.

That longevity matters. We're not a company that moved into the market during a remodeling boom and will move out when conditions change. Prescott is home. Our team members live here, their families live here, and our reputation in this community is something we protect every single day — one project at a time.

We hold Arizona ROC license #339999 and have maintained continuous licensure, bonding, and insurance throughout our history in the market. Our 5-star rating on Google reflects real homeowners in this community who trusted us with their homes.

We Support the Local Economy

Hiring local isn't just a tagline for us — it's how we operate. We prioritize Prescott-area subcontractors and suppliers wherever possible. When a project requires a licensed plumber, electrician, or HVAC technician, we go to the local trades we've built relationships with over the years — people whose work we know, whose standards meet ours, and who will be accountable to the same community you live in.

This approach keeps project dollars in Yavapai County. It supports local families in the trades. And it means that when a subcontractor does work in your home through us, they have a reputation here to protect too.

We also source materials regionally when it makes sense — working with local stone fabricators, regional cabinet suppliers with warehouses in the Phoenix metro area, and local tile distributors who understand the materials best suited for Prescott's high-elevation climate.

We Know Prescott Homes

Prescott's housing stock is distinct from the broader Arizona market. We work across a range of home types that are specific to this region:

  • 1970s–1980s ranch homes — often with galley kitchens, dated cabinetry, cast-iron plumbing, and tighter floor plans that require thoughtful layout planning
  • 1990s–2000s tract and semi-custom homes — common in Prescott Valley, often featuring builder-grade finishes ready for a quality update
  • Newer custom builds — across the Prescott area's expanding developments, where homeowners want premium finishes from the start
  • Historic homes near Courthouse Plaza — requiring sensitivity to original character while modernizing function

Beyond home types, we understand Prescott's climate realities. At 5,400 feet elevation, the Prescott area experiences freeze-thaw cycles that affect grout, tile adhesives, and outdoor surfaces. We spec materials and installation methods that account for this — not just what a Phoenix contractor might default to. We also understand Prescott's hard water (fed by a limestone aquifer) and how it affects stone countertop maintenance, fixture longevity, and shower grout over time.

Why a Local Contractor Outperforms a National Chain

National remodeling brands offer predictability — familiar branding, centralized scheduling, standardized packages. What they often can't offer is the thing that matters most: direct accountability to a real person in your community.

When you hire Infinity Kitchens and Baths, Steve Hunt is personally involved in the consultation, the design process, and project oversight. You have a direct line to the person responsible for your project — not a customer service number that routes to a call center in another state.

We live in the same community as our clients. Our kids go to the same schools. We shop at the same stores. Our neighbors are our clients. That dynamic creates a level of accountability that no corporate franchise structure can replicate — because our professional reputation and our personal reputation are the same thing.

If something isn't right on a project, we make it right — because the alternative is walking past that house every year and knowing we didn't. That's not how we operate.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long has Infinity Kitchens and Baths been in business?

Since 2011 — over a decade of remodeling in the Prescott, Arizona area. We've maintained continuous ROC licensure, bonding, and insurance throughout that time. Our longevity in this market is a direct reflection of the quality of our work and the trust our clients place in us.

Is Steve Hunt personally involved in projects?

Yes. As the founder and owner, Steve is directly involved in client consultations, project design, material selection, and oversight throughout construction. You're not dealing with a sales rep who hands your project off — you have a direct relationship with the person responsible for delivering results.

Do you use out-of-area subcontractors?

We prioritize Yavapai County-based subcontractors for plumbing, electrical, and HVAC work. Local tradespeople know local codes, have established relationships with local inspectors, and are accountable to the same community. Occasionally, specialized work or supply constraints require outside resources — but local-first is always the starting point.

Why does hiring a local contractor matter for a remodel?

Local contractors know local building codes, understand local inspector requirements, and have working relationships with local permit offices — all of which reduce delays. They understand the regional climate and which materials perform well at Prescott's elevation. And perhaps most importantly, a local contractor's reputation in the community is their primary business asset. That accountability doesn't exist for national chains whose crews rotate through on project-by-project contracts.
