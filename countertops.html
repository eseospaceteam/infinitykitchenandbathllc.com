Countertops

Countertops in Prescott, AZ

Every material compared, real installed costs for the Prescott market, and factory-direct fabrication without the retail showroom markup.

Quartz suits most Prescott kitchens because it never needs sealing and our hard water is unkind to anything that does. Granite if you want natural stone and heat resistance; quartzite for the marble look with real durability; butcher block for warmth on an island. Installed, expect roughly $55–$120 per square foot for quartz and $45–$100 for granite.

Below: the full cost table for all six materials, every material comparison on this site, and how to vet a fabricator.

Countertops are the surface people touch every day and the one they look at from every angle of the room, which is why they carry so much of a kitchen's finished feel. They are also the decision homeowners most often make twice — picking a material for how it looks in a showroom, then discovering what it asks of them once it is installed and living under real use.

The comparisons below exist to prevent that. Each one is written around the trade-off that actually matters for the material, not a feature list.

Common Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the best countertop material for a Prescott kitchen?

For most households, quartz. It is non-porous, so it never needs sealing — which matters here because hard water leaves mineral residue that shows on any surface needing a sealer to stay protected. Choose granite if you want natural stone with real heat resistance and do not mind sealing it annually. Choose quartzite if you want the look of marble with far more durability. Full comparisons: quartz vs granite and quartzite vs quartz.

How much do countertops cost installed?

Installed, per square foot: laminate $8–$18, butcher block $35–$65, granite $45–$100, quartz $55–$120, marble $75–$150 and quartzite $85–$165. For a typical 60-square-foot kitchen with an island that puts quartz around $3,300–$7,200 and granite around $2,700–$6,000. The full table with maintenance for each is on countertop costs.

Should I avoid marble in a kitchen?

Not necessarily, but go in with clear eyes. Marble etches from anything acidic — lemon, wine, vinegar, some cleaners — and that etching is a physical change to the stone, not a stain you can scrub out. It needs sealing several times a year. Plenty of people accept that because nothing else looks quite like it. If you love the look but not the upkeep, quartzite is the usual answer. See granite vs marble.

Do you template and fabricate, or subcontract it?

We handle the project end to end and source materials factory-direct rather than through a retail showroom, which is where a lot of the markup on stone sits. That is also why we can quote a fixed price: we know what the slab costs and what the fabrication costs before we give you a number.

Can I replace only the countertops and keep my cabinets?

Usually yes, and it is one of the highest-impact single upgrades in a kitchen. The cabinets need to be structurally sound and level, since stone will not forgive a cabinet run that has sagged. If your cabinets are solid but dated, look at refinishing or refacing alongside new tops — together they read as a full remodel at a fraction of the cost.

What about the backsplash?

A backsplash is normally part of a kitchen remodel and we handle it as part of the job — see kitchen backsplashes. If a backsplash on its own is all you need, we will refer you to a tile installer we trust rather than send a sales rep out for it. Everyone gets a better outcome that way.
