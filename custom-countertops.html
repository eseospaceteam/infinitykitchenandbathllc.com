Stone & Surface Options

Countertop Materials We Install in Prescott

No two homes — or homeowners — are the same. We carry six distinct surface families so you can match material to lifestyle, budget, and aesthetic with confidence.

Quartz

Engineered from 90–95% crushed natural quartz bound with polymer resins, quartz delivers unmatched consistency. It is non-porous — bacteria and stains cannot penetrate the surface — and it never needs sealing. With 100+ colors and patterns, including convincing marble and concrete looks, quartz adapts to every design direction. Most manufacturers back it with a 25-year residential warranty.

Granite

Pulled from quarries on every continent, granite is igneous rock — formed under intense heat and pressure over millions of years. Every slab is genuinely one-of-a-kind, with movement, color, and mineral deposits that cannot be replicated. Granite is heat- and scratch-resistant straight out of the box. Seal it once a year and it will outlast the house. An enduring choice for kitchens and bathroom vanities alike.

Quartzite

Often confused with quartz (the engineered product), quartzite is a 100% natural metamorphic stone — sandstone transformed by heat and pressure into one of the hardest materials available for countertops. It is harder than granite, carries a luminous marble-like appearance, and holds its polish beautifully. Quartzite requires sealing but rewards homeowners with a surface that ages gracefully for decades.

Marble

Few materials carry the cultural weight of marble. The soft, cool tone and dramatic veining have defined luxury interiors for centuries. Marble is softer than granite or quartzite and will etch from acidic liquids, which makes it a considered choice for high-traffic cooking surfaces — but a spectacular option for lower-traffic bathrooms, islands used for baking, and vanity tops where its beauty can shine without the daily abuse of a prep kitchen.

Porcelain Slabs

Large-format porcelain slabs — available up to 120"×60" — bring the look of natural stone with engineering-grade performance. UV stable and frost-resistant, porcelain is equally at home in an indoor kitchen or an outdoor cooking area. Ultra-thin profiles (just 6 mm) allow waterfall edges and low-profile applications that thicker stones cannot achieve. Maintenance is virtually zero.

Dekton / Sintered Stone

Sintered stone is next-generation surface technology — raw minerals compressed under extreme heat and pressure to produce a material with zero porosity, complete scratch resistance, and the ability to withstand temperatures that would damage any other countertop material. Dekton and similar sintered products are ideal for serious cooks who want a surface that performs as hard as it looks. Available in bold, sophisticated finishes unavailable in natural stone.

The Most Popular Choice in Prescott

Why Quartz is the #1 Choice for Prescott Kitchens

Year after year, quartz outpaces every other material in new kitchen installations across the country — and the Prescott market is no different. The reasons are practical: in a region where summer temperatures spike and kitchens see heavy use, homeowners want a surface that performs without constant maintenance. Quartz delivers exactly that.

Because it is engineered rather than quarried, quartz is free of the natural fissures and micro-pores that granite and marble carry. Spilled red wine, olive oil, tomato sauce — none of it penetrates. Wipe it up and move on. There is no annual sealing appointment, no worry about acidic foods etching the finish, no concern about bacteria harboring in invisible pores. For families with children, professional cooks, and homeowners who simply want a worry-free surface, quartz is the clear answer.

Arizona's desert heat creates one additional consideration: thermal shock from placing a hot pan directly on the countertop. While no countertop is truly "heatproof," quartz handles brief contact with warm cookware well under normal use. Pair it with the right kitchen cabinets and a well-planned kitchen layout and you have a surface that works as hard as you do for 25+ years.

Our Process

How We Install Your Countertops

01

In-Home Consultation & Material Selection

We visit your home, assess the existing layout, take preliminary measurements, and walk through your material options with actual samples in hand. You will see exactly how each stone looks under your lighting before you commit to anything. We discuss color, pattern, edge profile, and how the countertop fits into your broader kitchen or bathroom remodeling vision.

02

Precise Templating & CNC Fabrication

After material selection, we return for a detailed template session. Every dimension is captured digitally and converted to a CNC fabrication file. Your slab is then cut, shaped, and polished at our facility to exact specification — including sink cutouts, cooktop openings, and any custom features such as waterfall edges or integrated drainboards.

03

Cabinet & Sink Prep

Before the countertop arrives, we confirm that your cabinet boxes are level and structurally sound. If you are combining countertops with new kitchen cabinets or bathroom vanities, this stage ensures the substrate is perfectly plumb and ready to support the stone. Old countertops are carefully removed and debris is cleared from the work area.

04

Countertop Installation & Seaming

Slabs are transported on padded carriers and set into place with precision. Seams are joined with color-matched epoxy and polished flush so they nearly disappear into the stone's natural pattern. Undermount sinks are clipped from below. We level the entire run to within 1/16" and verify every overhang is consistent before epoxy cures.

05

Edge Polish, Caulk & Final Inspection

All exposed edges receive a final hand-polish. Backsplash and sink perimeters are caulked with color-matched silicone. We do a full walk-through with you, pointing out every seam, cutout, and edge so you can inspect the work up close. No surprises, no punch-list items left for another day. Part of our commitment as Prescott's full-service kitchen remodeling team.

Common Questions

Countertop FAQ

How long does countertop installation take in Prescott?
For a typical kitchen, the process from initial consultation to completed installation runs 10–14 business days. The consultation and template take 1–2 visits; fabrication at our facility takes 5–7 business days; installation itself is usually a single day. Larger projects with multiple rooms — for example, combining a kitchen and two bathrooms — may add a few days. We give you a firm timeline before any work begins.
What is the difference between quartz and quartzite?
Quartz (the countertop product) is an engineered material — natural quartz crystals ground up and bound with resin. It is manufactured in controlled conditions, which gives it consistent color and pattern and eliminates porosity. Quartzite is a 100% natural metamorphic rock quarried from the earth. It has a marble-like appearance but is harder and more durable than marble. Quartzite requires periodic sealing; quartz does not. Both are excellent materials — the right choice depends on your maintenance preferences and the aesthetic you are going for.
Does granite need to be sealed?
Yes. Granite is a natural stone with microscopic pores that can absorb liquids over time if left unsealed. We seal all granite countertops at the time of installation. After that, we recommend resealing once a year — a simple DIY process that takes about 20 minutes. Many modern granite sealers last 18–24 months. We are happy to walk you through the process at the end of your installation so you know exactly what to do when the time comes.
Can I keep my existing cabinets and just replace the countertops?
Absolutely. Countertop-only replacement is one of our most common standalone services. As long as your cabinet boxes are structurally sound and level, we can template and install new stone countertops without touching the rest of the kitchen. It is one of the highest-impact, most cost-effective upgrades available to Prescott homeowners — transforming the look of the entire kitchen or bathroom for a fraction of a full remodel cost.
What edge profiles do you offer?
We offer a full library of standard and decorative edge profiles, including: eased (the clean, contemporary square edge), beveled (a 45-degree chamfer on the top corner), bullnose (fully rounded top edge), half-bullnose, ogee (an S-curve classical profile), waterfall (slab continues vertically to the floor on islands), and mitered (for thick-look edges on thinner slabs). We bring edge samples to the consultation so you can feel the profile in person before choosing.
