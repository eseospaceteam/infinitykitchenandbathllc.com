Groutless vs. Tile Showers: Which Is Right for Your Avondale Bath?

By Steve Hunt, Owner & Lead Designer at Infinity Kitchens and Baths

Reviewed by Steve Hunt, Owner & Lead Designer · AZ ROC #339999 · Last reviewed November 20, 2026

Updated November 20, 2026 • 13 min read

Weighing shower types? Our complete shower guide compares walk-in, tub-to-shower, curbless and groutless side by side, with real installed costs.

Short answer: Choose groutless panels if you want the least cleaning and a faster, usually cheaper install: on our projects a panel shower runs about $1,000 to $2,500 less than comparable tile and goes in within one to two days. Choose tile if you want unlimited design choices and will keep up with the grout.

When clients contact Infinity Kitchens and Baths about a new shower, the conversation almost always starts the same way: "I want tile." It makes sense. Tile is what most people grew up with, and a beautiful custom tile shower is genuinely stunning when it is done right. But over the last decade, groutless shower wall panels have become a serious alternative, and for a growing number of homeowners, a smarter one.

This is not a knock on tile. We install a lot of tile showers, and we are proud of every one of them. But we also know from years of building and repairing showers that grout is the weak point of most tile showers, and living with tile is often more demanding than homeowners expect. Groutless panels solve that problem in a way tile cannot.

This guide gives you an honest, side-by-side look at both options: what each one is, how they compare on the factors that matter most, what they typically cost, and which type of homeowner is the best fit for each.

What Is a Groutless Shower?

A groutless shower uses large wall panels in place of individual tiles. The panels are made from composite, solid surface, acrylic, cultured marble or large-format porcelain, and they are finished to look like stone, marble, concrete or large-format tile, without grout lines between them.

Infinity Kitchens and Baths is an authorized Sentrel dealer. Sentrel builds its panels from bonded layers on a high-density PVC core, 1/4" thick by its own spec (about twice to three times standard acrylic sheet), with the surface printed from high-resolution scans of real stone slabs. The line includes more than 20 stone colors, from marble and granite to travertine looks, plus tile-look SimTile patterns. Because each panel covers a whole wall section, a shower has a handful of sealed seams instead of hundreds of grout joints.

Installation is faster than tile because there is no setting mortar, no tile layout and no grout cure. The panels are bonded to a prepared backer, and the seams are sealed with color-matched silicone. The result looks like a stone or large-format tile shower, with no grout to clean, seal or repair. For every panel material and its pros and cons, see our guide to groutless shower walls.

Learn more about our groutless shower systems and the Sentrel products we carry.

What Is a Tile Shower?

A tile shower uses individual ceramic, porcelain or natural stone tiles set in thinset mortar on a waterproofed substrate, with grout filling the joints between each tile. Tile gives you nearly unlimited creative freedom: any size, shape, color, finish or layout. Subway tile, large-format 24×24 porcelain, mosaic glass, herringbone natural stone and wood-look plank tile are all possible.

The critical variable in any tile shower is waterproofing. Tile and grout do not keep water out; the waterproofing membrane underneath does. Mapei's guide to wet-area tile puts it plainly: water and moisture get through the tile installation and have to be carried by the backer, membrane and shower pan to the drain. At Infinity, we use Schluter Systems products, Kerdi membrane or Kerdi-Board, to create a fully bonded waterproof barrier behind every tile shower we install, so moisture does not reach the studs or framing even as the grout ages.

A properly waterproofed, professionally installed tile shower is a long-lasting, beautiful addition to any home. The challenge is maintenance, specifically the grout. We come back to that below.

Here is how the two options stack up on the factors that matter most to homeowners:

Factor Groutless (Sentrel Panels) Tile (Porcelain / Stone)
Installation time 1–2 days 7–14 days (set, grout, cure, seal)
Cost installed (our projects) $3,500–$6,500 $4,500–$9,000+
Maintenance No grout to seal or scrub; squeegee and wipe Cement grout needs regular scrubbing and resealing; epoxy grout needs no sealing
Warranty in writing Sentrel lifetime product warranty Varies by tile product; grout and caulk not typically warrantied
Customization 20+ stone colors plus tile-look patterns Unlimited: every size, color, texture and layout
Repairability Difficult to repair a single damaged panel Individual tile replacement possible
Mold risk Minimal: no grout joints to harbor growth Higher: cement grout is porous and holds moisture
Where leaks start Silicone seams and fixture openings; keep them sealed The membrane behind the tile; grout is not waterproof

The Grout Problem, and Why It Matters in Avondale

Standard grout is a cement-based material. It is porous, so it soaks up water, soap residue, body oils, mildew spores and minerals with every shower. Over time those stain and discolor the grout, and once the staining is deep, no amount of scrubbing fully restores it. This is not a product defect or an installation error; it is what cement grout does in a wet room every day.

Sealing slows the process but does not stop it, and sealers wear off and have to be reapplied (our shower grout guide covers how often). Even careful homeowners skip it. Epoxy grout avoids the problem: LATICRETE says its epoxy grouts "never require sealing" because they absorb less than 0.5% water. It costs more and takes more skill to install, which is why it is not the default on most tile jobs.

In Avondale, the problem is compounded by water quality. The city's tap water is a blend of city well water and Colorado River (CAP) water, and it is very hard: the City of Avondale's 2022 water quality report put city-produced water at an average of about 21 grains per gallon (357 mg/L), with total dissolved solids averaging about 649 ppm. Mineral scale from water that hard builds up on grout faster than it does in soft-water areas, and it speeds up the discoloration of grout joints. With water like this, grout often looks good for a shorter time than the maintenance schedule suggests.

The bigger risk is hidden. Grout is not a waterproof layer, so a tile shower is only as watertight as the membrane behind it. When that membrane is missing or poorly built, water that passes through the grout reaches the wall cavity, and by the time damage shows on the outside, the framing and substrate are often already compromised. Panels take grout joints out of the picture, and a membrane built to the manufacturer's method takes the risk out of tile.

Weekly Cleaning: Groutless vs Tile Side by Side

Maintenance is where the two options differ most. This is what each one asks of you, based on the panel maker's care guide, the grout maker's instructions and our own grout guide:

Task Groutless panels (Sentrel) Tile, cement grout Tile, epoxy grout
After each shower Squeegee or towel-dry Squeegee; run the fan so grout dries Squeegee
Regular clean At least every two weeks, soft cloth and a mild spray Weekly, with a brush on the joints Weekly wipe-down
Hard-water spots Lime-A-Way or Tilex spray, soft sponge Scale settles into the joints and stains light grout Wipes off the joint surface
Sealing Never Reseal roughly once a year Never
Watch for Silicone at corners and fixtures pulling away Cracked or crumbling grout, loose caulk at corners Caulk at corners and changes of plane
Never use Magic Erasers, Comet, scouring pads, solvents Acidic cleaners on cement grout or natural stone Harsh abrasives on the tile face

Who Should Choose a Groutless Shower?

Groutless shower panels are the right choice for a specific type of homeowner, and if you fall into one of these groups, the decision is usually straightforward.

  • Homeowners who dislike cleaning showers. If regular grout scrubbing is something you know you will not keep up with, a groutless panel shower removes the problem. Maintenance comes down to a squeegee after use and a wipe-down every week or two, with no scrubbing and no sealing.
  • Vacation rental owners. Short-term rentals see heavy, inconsistent use. A groutless shower needs no grout care between guest stays and holds up to high-volume use better than a tile shower whose grout needs constant attention.
  • Older and mobility-limited homeowners. Reaching into a shower to scrub grout lines is physically demanding. For clients who are aging in place or dealing with mobility limits, the low-maintenance profile of groutless panels is a genuine quality-of-life improvement. See our aging-in-place remodeling page for more on this.
  • Budget-conscious renovators focused on total cost of ownership. The upfront cost of a groutless shower is usually lower than a custom tile shower, and there is no grout sealer or regrouting to pay for later. Over a ten-year window, the total cost usually favors groutless.
  • One-bathroom households and rental turnovers. When installation speed matters, because you only have one shower or you need a rental back on the market, the one-to-two day panel installation is a practical advantage over the one to two weeks tile takes.

Who Should Choose a Tile Shower?

Tile is the right answer in certain situations, and when it is the right answer, there is nothing better. Here is when we recommend tile:

  • Homeowners who want full design customization. No groutless panel system can match the creative range of tile. If you have a specific vision, such as a particular stone, a custom layout, a mixed-material design or a specific grout color, tile is the only way to get it.
  • Homeowners who are willing and able to maintain the grout. If you are disciplined about resealing and regular cleaning, or you choose epoxy grout, tile can look beautiful for many years. The key word is discipline. If that is realistic for you, tile is a rewarding choice.
  • Luxury master bathrooms where visual impact is the priority. A large-format marble tile shower with custom niches, a bench and a rain head is a design statement that groutless panels cannot fully replicate. In a high-end master bath where looks are the primary driver, custom tile is often worth the investment and the upkeep.
  • Projects where Schluter waterproofing is specified. When proper waterproofing is built in from the start, the water-damage risk of tile drops significantly. We use Schluter Kerdi or Kerdi-Board on every tile shower we build, which is why our tile showers hold up far better than tile installed without a proper waterproofing system. See our tile shower installation and walk-in shower pages for examples.

What Are the Downsides of Each?

Neither option is perfect. The honest list:

  • Groutless downsides: a set color palette; silicone seams that need an eye kept on them; finishes that abrasive cleaners can dull (Sentrel says off-limits cleaners can void its warranty); and a damaged panel usually means replacing that wall. More detail is in our groutless shower walls guide.
  • Tile downsides: porous cement grout that stains, needs resealing and eventually regrouting; a longer, messier installation with cure time; a higher price for custom work; and a shower that depends entirely on the waterproofing you cannot see.

Groutless Shower Cost vs Tile: Typical Prices

Pricing varies with shower size, material selection, plumbing scope, and whether the project involves demo and substrate repair. The figures below are typical installed costs on our projects for a standard remodel with no major surprises. They include labor, materials and standard trim. They do not include plumbing fixture upgrades, custom niches, or structural repairs if water damage is found.

Shower Size Groutless (Sentrel) Tile (Porcelain, Schluter WP)
Standard 3×5 ft shower $3,500–$5,000 $4,500–$7,000
Walk-in 4×6 ft shower $5,000–$6,500 $6,500–$9,000+

Are groutless showers expensive?

Not compared with custom tile. On our projects a panel shower usually costs $1,000 to $2,500 less than a comparable tile shower, mostly because tile labor takes longer. For a national view, Angi's 2026 data puts the average shower remodel at $7,000, with most homeowners spending $2,000 to $9,000, and prices tiling a shower at $1,800 to $6,800 on its own. Angi lists solid surface wall material at $40 to $70 per square foot and porcelain tile at $0.50 to $30, so material alone can favor tile; it is the labor and time that tip the total toward panels.

One more cost factor: material lead times affect scheduling. Panels generally have more predictable availability than specialty tile, which can run into delays when ordered from out-of-state suppliers. If your timeline is firm, that is another practical point for groutless.

Infinity's Honest Recommendation

We offer both groutless and tile showers, and we do not have a business preference for one over the other. We want to install the right product for each client's situation, not the one that is easiest to sell.

Our honest assessment, based on years of building both: if you do not have a strong preference for a specific tile look, and you are honest with yourself about how much time you want to spend on shower maintenance, groutless panels are often the smarter choice. They handle Avondale's very hard water well, there is no grout to degrade, and the lower installation cost plus minimal upkeep gives them a favorable total cost of ownership over a 10-to-15 year horizon.

If you want a truly custom tile design, such as a specific stone, a unique layout, or a master bath that makes a visual statement, tile is absolutely the way to go, and we will install it correctly with Schluter waterproofing so it performs as long as possible.

The best way to decide is to see both options in person. We bring Sentrel panel samples and tile samples to your home consultation so you can see the actual material, feel the surface, and compare them side by side in your own bathroom light. That is how most of our clients make their final decision.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can groutless panels look as good as tile?

For most homeowners, yes, especially from a few feet away. Sentrel prints its panel surfaces from high-resolution scans of real stone slabs, and many guests would not immediately identify the walls as panels. Up close, a skilled eye can tell panel seams from grout lines. If your priority is a high-end look with minimal maintenance, groutless panels deliver it. If you want a one-of-a-kind tile installation with custom layout and material choices, tile will always have the edge on pure looks.

Is groutless more expensive than tile?

Not typically. In most of our projects, a groutless panel shower runs $1,000 to $2,500 less than a comparable tile shower on the initial installation, largely because tile labor is more intensive and tile projects take longer with cure time factored in. When you add ongoing grout costs, such as sealer, grout cleaners and eventual regrouting or tile repairs over 10 to 15 years, the difference usually widens in favor of groutless.

Are Sentrel panels durable?

Yes. Sentrel panels are 1/4" thick with a high-density PVC core that the maker says resists flex, cracking and impact, and Sentrel backs them with a lifetime warranty. The surface is non-porous, so it does not absorb water, soap or minerals the way grout does. Like any panel, the finish can be dulled by abrasive cleaners, and Sentrel says using off-limits products can void the warranty, so stick to the cleaners it lists.

Can I use groutless panels for a shower floor?

In most cases, no. Shower wall panels are designed for walls. Shower floors need a textured, slip-resistant surface that can handle standing loads, foot traffic and drainage, which flat wall panels do not provide. In groutless shower builds the floor is typically a matching or complementary tile or a prefabricated shower base. We will walk you through the flooring options during your consultation.

How do I clean a groutless shower?

Squeegee or towel-dry the walls after showering, and give the whole shower a full clean at least every two weeks with a soft sponge or cloth. Sentrel's care guide lists everyday sprays such as Windex, Fantastik, 409, Simple Green and Pine-Sol, and Lime-A-Way or Tilex spray for hard-water spots and soap scum, which matters with Avondale's very hard water. Never use Magic Erasers, Comet or other powder cleansers, scouring pads, or solvents. No sealing, no grout scrubbing, no annual treatments.

What is the best material for a walk-in shower?

For low maintenance, groutless panels on the walls with a matching shower base or an epoxy-grouted tile floor. For full design freedom, porcelain tile over a bonded waterproofing membrane. A curbless walk-in needs a floor that slopes to the drain, so the floor is tile or a low-profile base either way; see our walk-in showers page for how we build them.

Can groutless panels go over existing tile?

Sometimes, if the old tile is flat, solid and dry, but we generally recommend removing it. Water damage hiding behind old tile is common, and covering it just traps the problem. Removing the tile lets us check the framing and put up a fresh backer before the panels go on.
