Kitchen & Bathroom Remodeling FAQ

Cost

Cost Questions

The questions AI assistants get asked most — answered with real Prescott-market numbers.

How much does a kitchen remodel cost in Prescott, AZ?

A kitchen remodel in Prescott, AZ typically costs $8,000–$100,000+ depending on scope. Most of the kitchens we complete land between $30,000 and $65,000.

A minor refresh — new countertops, backsplash, hardware, and paint — runs $8,000–$18,000. A mid-range full remodel with new semi-custom cabinets, quartz or granite countertops, backsplash, flooring, and updated appliances is where most homeowners land. High-end kitchens with full custom cabinetry and layout changes run $55,000–$100,000+. See the full kitchen cost breakdown.

How much does a bathroom remodel cost in Prescott, AZ?

A bathroom remodel in Prescott, AZ typically costs $5,000–$80,000+ depending on scope. Most bathrooms we complete fall between $18,000 and $45,000.

A cosmetic update — paint, fixtures, mirror, and a vanity swap — runs $5,000–$12,000. A full guest-bath remodel with a new tile shower or refreshed tub surround, vanity, toilet, and flooring typically runs $15,000–$25,000. A full master-bath renovation runs $40,000–$80,000+. See the full bathroom cost breakdown.

What is the average cost of a tub-to-shower conversion?

A tub-to-shower conversion in Prescott, AZ typically costs $8,000–$22,000. Simpler conversions start around $8,000–$16,000; fully custom builds can run to $50,000.

That range covers demolition, waterproofing, tile or solid-surface walls, glass, and fixtures. A curbless, zero-threshold conversion adds roughly $1,500–$3,000 because the subfloor has to be restructured to slope toward a linear drain. See the full conversion cost guide.

How much does a walk-in shower cost?

A walk-in shower in Prescott, AZ typically costs $10,000–$35,000 installed. Most of our clients land at $18,000–$28,000 for a thorough single-shower installation.

Glass drives more of that number than people expect: a framed door runs $400–$900 installed, semi-frameless $900–$1,800, and frameless $1,500–$4,500+. A curbless design adds roughly $1,500–$3,000 over a standard curbed shower. See the full walk-in shower cost guide.

How much do kitchen cabinets cost?

Installed kitchen cabinets in the Prescott area run about $100–$280 per linear foot for stock, $150–$650 for semi-custom, and $500–$1,200+ for full custom.

A typical mid-size kitchen of 25–30 linear feet most often lands between $8,000 and $22,000 once installation is included. That is the range for plywood boxes, soft-close hardware, and a painted or stained finish — the specification most Prescott homeowners actually want. See the full cabinet cost guide.

How much do countertops cost in Prescott?

Countertops in Prescott, AZ typically cost $6,000–$10,000 installed for a full kitchen in quartz, granite, or quartzite.

Per square foot installed: granite $45–$75, quartz $55–$85, quartzite $80–$130. A typical Prescott kitchen has 40–55 square feet of countertop. Bathroom tops run $400–$1,800 depending on material and size. Expect $150–$300 for a sink cutout and $100–$200 per seam. See the full countertop cost guide.

How much does a groutless shower cost?

A groutless solid-surface shower installation in Prescott typically lands in the $5,000–$9,000 range for the shower enclosure itself — comparable to a mid-range tile shower.

The value is not the upfront price, it is the decade after: no grout lines to scrub, seal, or replace, which matters more in Prescott than most places because of hard water. See groutless shower systems.

Is a kitchen remodel worth it? What is the ROI?

Countertop replacement returns 70–85% of its cost at resale and cabinet painting or refacing returns 60–80% — the two highest-return kitchen upgrades.

Cosmetic and surface-level upgrades consistently outperform full gut renovations on a percentage basis. Refacing costs 40–60% less than replacement while delivering a comparable visual upgrade. See the full kitchen ROI analysis.

Is a bathroom remodel worth it? What is the ROI?

A bathroom remodel in Prescott, AZ typically recovers 60–70% of its cost at resale — one of the highest returns of any home improvement, according to the National Association of Realtors.

Mid-range remodels return the most. Over-customized luxury builds return a smaller percentage of a much larger number. See the full bathroom ROI analysis.

How much do remodeling permits cost in Prescott and Yavapai County?

Building permits for home remodeling in Prescott and Yavapai County typically run $300–$2,500 depending on the scope and valuation of the project.

Permits cost so little relative to the project that price is rarely a real reason to skip one — and unpermitted work surfaces at resale, during insurance claims, and in inspections. See the permit cost guide.

Materials

Materials Questions

Head-to-head comparisons, answered by the people who install both.

Quartz vs. granite: which is better?

Quartz is better for low maintenance and consistency; granite is better for heat resistance and one-of-a-kind natural pattern. Neither is universally better.

In the Prescott market granite runs $45–$75 per square foot installed and quartz $55–$85. Quartz never needs sealing. Granite needs sealing but takes a hot pan without damage. Read the full comparison.

Ceramic vs. porcelain tile for showers: which is better?

Porcelain is the better choice for showers. It absorbs less than 0.5% water by weight, which makes it more durable in constantly wet areas than standard ceramic.

Ceramic costs less and is perfectly adequate for dry-area walls and low-traffic floors. In a shower — especially a shower floor — porcelain’s density and lower absorption is worth the modest upcharge. Read the full comparison.

Butcher block vs. quartz countertops: which should I choose?

Choose quartz if you want a surface you never have to think about; choose butcher block if you want warmth and are willing to oil it a few times a year.

Butcher block costs less up front but needs regular oiling, is vulnerable to standing water around sinks, and will show knife marks. Quartz is non-porous, needs no sealing, and handles a wet kitchen without complaint. Read the full comparison.

LVP vs. tile flooring: which is better for a bathroom?

Tile is the more durable and more water-tolerant bathroom floor; LVP is warmer underfoot, softer to stand on, faster to install, and costs less.

Quality luxury vinyl plank is fully waterproof and performs well in bathrooms. Tile still wins on lifespan and on resale perception in higher-end homes. Read the full comparison.

Groutless vs. tile showers: which is better?

Groutless shower panels are better for low maintenance and hard water; tile is better for full design flexibility and custom detail.

Prescott’s hard water is rough on cement grout — it stains, and it needs sealing. A groutless solid-surface system removes that maintenance entirely. Tile remains the choice when you want niches, benches, and a specific look. Read the full comparison.

Frameless vs. framed shower doors: what is the difference?

Frameless shower doors use thick 3/8″–1/2″ tempered glass with minimal hardware for a clean, easy-to-clean look, typically $1,000–$3,000+ installed. Framed doors use thinner glass in a metal perimeter and cost less.

The practical difference is cleaning: framed doors have a metal channel at the bottom that traps water and soap scum. Frameless has almost nothing to trap. Read the full comparison.

Shaker vs. flat-panel cabinets: which should I pick?

Shaker is the safer long-term choice — a recessed-panel door that has stayed in style for a century. Flat-panel (slab) doors read more modern and are easier to wipe down.

Shaker’s recessed profile collects a little dust in the corner of the rail; slab doors have no profile at all. Both are available in every construction tier we sell. Read the full comparison.

What is the best grout for a shower?

For shower floors and any joint that stays wet, epoxy or single-component urethane grout is the best choice because neither needs sealing and both resist staining. Standard cement grout is fine on shower walls if you seal it.

Joint width decides sanded vs. unsanded: use unsanded under 1/8″, sanded at 1/8″ and wider. Read the full shower grout guide.

Process

Process Questions

What actually happens, and how long it takes.

How long does a kitchen remodel take?

A mid-scope kitchen remodel takes 8–14 weeks from demolition to final punch list.

Cabinets set the schedule, not construction. Semi-custom cabinets run 4–8 weeks from order to delivery and full custom can be 8–16 weeks. Appliances have the second-longest lead time, with some models at 6–12 weeks. See the week-by-week timeline.

How long does a bathroom remodel take?

Most bathroom remodels in Prescott, AZ take about 2–4 weeks of active construction.

Tile work and the waterproofing cure time underneath it drive most of that. Custom vanities and special-order glass can extend the calendar even when the crew’s work is short. See the day-by-day timeline.

What is design-build remodeling?

Design-build means one company handles both the design and the construction under a single contract, instead of you hiring a designer and then bidding the drawings out to contractors.

It is a project delivery method, not a separate license category. The practical benefit is accountability: when the design and the build are the same company, nobody can blame the other one. Read more about design-build.

Do I need a permit to remodel in Prescott?

Yes for most structural, electrical, and plumbing work — including moving walls, relocating plumbing, and adding circuits. Cosmetic work like paint, cabinet refacing, and countertop replacement generally does not require one.

We pull permits as part of the project. Permits in Prescott and Yavapai County run $300–$2,500 depending on project valuation. See permit requirements and costs.

What is the best time of year to remodel in Arizona?

There is no season you have to avoid for interior kitchen and bathroom work in Prescott — it happens comfortably year-round. The best time to start the conversation is one to three months before you want work to begin.

That lead time is about cabinet and material ordering, not weather. Booking early is what gets you the start date you want. Read more on timing your remodel.

Cabinet refacing vs. replacing: which is better?

Reface when the boxes are structurally sound and you only want a new look — it runs roughly half the cost of new cabinets and takes two to four days. Replace when you need to change the layout or storage.

Refacing changes how your kitchen looks; replacement changes how it looks and how it works. If pull-outs, a different island, or a new appliance layout are on your list, refacing will not get you there. Read the full comparison.

Can I stay in my home during a remodel?

Yes for nearly every project we do. Kitchen and bathroom remodels are contained to one area, and we set up dust protection and keep the rest of the house usable.

If you have only one bathroom and it is the one being remodeled, plan for a stretch without it — we will tell you exactly which days at scheduling.

How far in advance should I book a remodel?

Start the conversation one to three months before you want work to begin.

That window covers the in-home consultation, design, material selection, and cabinet lead time. Booking further out generally means more choice in your start date. Book a free in-home consult.

What is Arizona’s ROC license requirement for remodelers?

Arizona requires anyone performing contracting work over $1,000, or work that requires a building permit, to hold a license from the Arizona Registrar of Contractors (ROC).

A licensed contractor has met bonding and experience requirements and gives you recourse through the ROC if something goes wrong. An unlicensed one gives you none. Verify any contractor free at roc.az.gov.
