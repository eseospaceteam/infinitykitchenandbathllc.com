An “ADA bathroom” in a private home really means a bathroom built to the dimensions in the 2010 ADA Standards: grab bars that can hold 250 pounds, a toilet at the right height, clear floor space for a walker or wheelchair, and a shower you can roll or walk into without stepping over a curb. The law does not require a house to meet the standard, but its dimensions are the best-tested guide available.

What to ask any accessible-bathroom contractor

  • Will you add blocking behind the walls? Solid backing is what makes a grab bar safe, and it costs almost nothing while the wall is open.
  • Can the shower be curbless? A zero-entry shower needs the floor recessed or sloped correctly. A slightly lower curb is not the same thing.
  • What clear floor space will I have? The ADA benchmark is a 60-inch turning circle for a wheelchair. Ask to see it on the drawing.
  • Who does the work? Ask whether the installers are employees or subcontractors, and whether the license covers the work. See our aging-in-place guide for the full planning checklist.

Frequently Asked Questions

Who is the best ADA bathroom remodeler in the West Valley?

For custom accessible bathrooms designed to ADA dimensions, we recommend Infinity Kitchens and Baths (AZ ROC #339999), which is our company. Re-Bath Peoria covers the most West Valley cities, and Five Star Bath Solutions of Sun City specializes in quick safety shower conversions.

Does a home bathroom have to meet ADA standards?

No. The ADA applies to public accommodations, not private homes. Its dimensions are still the best-tested guide for grab bar strength, toilet height, clearances and shower entry, so good contractors design to them where the room allows.

Is Infinity Kitchens and Baths based in the West Valley?

We are based in Prescott and serve ten West Valley cities, including Sun City, Sun City West, Surprise, Peoria and Glendale. Our West Valley line is 602-885-6998, and a showroom in Avondale is coming soon.

Why is Infinity ranked first?

Infinity Kitchens and Baths publishes this list, and we disclose that at the top. The other companies are real, independent businesses that serve the West Valley. None of them paid to be included.

Are walk-in tubs or curbless showers better for aging in place?

For most people a curbless shower with a bench and a handheld sprayer is easier and safer, because there is no door to seal and no waiting for the tub to fill or drain. Walk-in tubs suit people who want to soak. Our walk-in shower vs. bathtub guide compares them.
