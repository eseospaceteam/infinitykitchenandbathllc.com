Whole House Remodeling Prescott, AZ

One Contractor. One Vision. One Seamless Project.

Whole-home remodeling projects fail most often not because of bad craftsmanship, but because of poor coordination. When a homeowner hires separate contractors for the kitchen, bathrooms, flooring, and structural work, each trade operates on its own schedule, with its own material choices and its own definition of "done." The result is a home that feels like a patchwork of unrelated projects — mismatched finishes, scheduling gaps that stretch a 3-month job into 18 months, and endless arguments over whose responsibility a problem falls under.

Infinity Kitchen and Bath serves as the single point of accountability for every trade involved in your whole-home remodel. From the first design consultation through the final punch list, one team manages every subcontractor, every material order, every permit, and every inspection. When you have a question, you call one number. When something needs to change, one person makes the call. That clarity of ownership is what separates a smooth renovation from a nightmare.

Before a single wall comes down, our CAD design process lets you see the full vision for every room in your home. We produce detailed 3D renderings of your kitchen, bathrooms, and living spaces so you can review finishes, layouts, and material choices as a cohesive whole — not room by room. Explore all our home remodeling services to understand the full scope of what we offer under one roof.

The Case for One Contractor on a Whole-Home Project

When homeowners hire separate contractors for each phase of a whole-home remodel, scheduling conflicts are almost guaranteed. The flooring installer can't start until the bathrooms are tiled. The tiler can't start until the rough plumbing passes inspection. The plumber reschedules twice because the framing sub ran over. Each delay compounds the next, and what was planned as a 4-month project stretches well past a year. In the meantime, the homeowner is fielding calls from five different contractors, each claiming the holdup is someone else's fault.

Material inconsistencies are the other hidden cost of the multi-contractor approach. When a kitchen contractor orders quartz, a flooring contractor orders LVP, and a bathroom contractor orders tile in separate phases with no shared design direction, the finishes rarely speak to each other. You end up with a warm-toned kitchen, cool-toned bathrooms, and a flooring color that bridges neither — a problem that is expensive and disruptive to correct after the fact.

Infinity Kitchen and Bath eliminates the finger-pointing entirely. As the single general contractor on your whole-home project, we own every outcome. If there is a scheduling issue, we resolve it internally. If a material is backordered, we find an alternative and communicate the change to you — not to a chain of four separate contractors who each need to agree. Our clients consistently describe the experience as dramatically less stressful than previous multi-contractor projects. See the full range of our remodeling services in Prescott.

How We Manage Your Whole House Remodel

01

Full-Home Assessment & Scope Planning

Every whole-home project begins with a thorough walkthrough of your property. We assess the current condition of every room, identify any structural, mechanical, or code issues that need to be addressed, and document your goals for each space. From this assessment we build a detailed scope of work that covers every trade, every room, and every finish decision — so there are no surprises once work begins. This phase typically takes one to two meetings.

02

3D CAD Design — Every Room, Every Detail

Our in-house design team produces 3D computer-aided design renderings for every room included in your project. You will see your new kitchen layout, bathroom configurations, flooring patterns, and finish selections as a unified vision before a single demo cut is made. This step is where whole-home projects succeed or fail — getting every design decision approved before construction begins eliminates the costly mid-project changes that inflate budgets and extend timelines. Changes are easy on a screen; they are expensive in a wall.

03

Permits, Material Ordering & Scheduling

Once designs are approved, we pull all required permits for every scope of work — structural, plumbing, electrical, and mechanical. Simultaneously, we place material orders for all cabinetry, countertop slabs, flooring, tile, and fixtures. Ordering everything at once allows us to source complementary materials and lock in factory-direct pricing on bulk quantities. Our project manager then builds a full construction schedule sequencing every trade in the correct order, with lead times for material delivery built in.

04

Structural, Mechanical & Rough-In Work

Demolition and rough-in work begins according to the approved schedule. If your project includes load-bearing wall removal, beam installation, or ceiling work, that structural scope is completed first. Plumbing, electrical, and HVAC rough-in follows — all inspected and approved before walls are closed. We coordinate all subcontractors on-site and conduct daily quality checks to ensure rough-in work meets our standards before finishes begin. You have one point of contact throughout this phase.

05

Finishes — Flooring, Tile, Cabinets, Countertops

With rough-in work approved, finish installation begins in the correct sequence: flooring, then tile, then cabinetry, then countertop fabrication and installation, then fixtures and hardware. Installing in this sequence protects finishes from damage by later trades and ensures that transitions between materials are clean and intentional. Our crews install flooring throughout the entire home in a single mobilization, eliminating the seam issues that arise when flooring is installed in phases. Cabinet installation, countertop templating, and final fixture connections follow on a tightly managed schedule.

06

Punch List, Final Inspection & Warranty

Before we consider a project complete, we conduct an internal punch list walkthrough with your project manager — checking every room, every finish, every fixture, and every transition against the approved design. You then walk through the home with us and add anything you want addressed. All punch list items are resolved before final payment. We obtain final building inspections for all permitted work and provide written warranty documentation covering our workmanship. Your home should be exactly what you approved in the design phase — and we do not consider the job done until it is.

Whole House Remodeling FAQ

How long does a whole house remodel take in Prescott?
Timeline depends heavily on the scope of the project. A focused whole-home update — new flooring throughout, kitchen refresh, one or two bathroom remodels — typically takes 3 to 4 months with our coordinated single-contractor approach. A full gut renovation involving structural work, multiple bathroom remodels, a complete kitchen overhaul, and new flooring throughout a larger home may take 5 to 6 months. We provide a project-specific timeline at the end of the design phase, before any work begins, so you know exactly what to expect.
Do I need to move out during a whole house remodel?
For partial whole-home projects — where work is concentrated in specific areas of the home — many clients choose to stay. For full gut renovations involving kitchen demolition, multiple bathroom tear-outs, and flooring removal throughout the home, temporary relocation is strongly recommended. Living in an active construction zone is not only uncomfortable but can slow the project as crews work around occupants. We discuss living arrangements during our initial scope planning meeting so you can make an informed decision based on your specific project.
How do you phase a whole-home remodel to minimize disruption?
We build a phased construction schedule that groups demolition and rough-in work together and sequences finish installation from the back of the home toward the front, or from secondary spaces toward primary living areas. This approach allows portions of the home to become habitable sooner while work continues elsewhere. Structural and mechanical work is front-loaded so inspections do not create downstream delays. Our project manager reviews the phasing plan with you before construction begins so you understand what will be livable and when throughout the project.
How much does a whole house remodel cost in Prescott?
Whole-home remodel costs in Prescott vary widely based on the size of the home, the number of rooms involved, the level of finish selected, and whether structural work is included. A focused whole-home update — flooring throughout, kitchen remodel, and two bathrooms — in a 1,500 to 2,000 square foot home typically starts in the $80,000–$120,000 range. Full gut renovations of larger homes with structural changes and high-end finishes can reach $200,000 or more. We provide a detailed, itemized estimate after the design phase is complete so you know exactly what every dollar covers. Contact us for a free consultation and preliminary budget discussion.
Do you handle permits for every trade?
Yes. As the general contractor of record on your whole-home project, we pull all required permits for every scope of work — including structural, plumbing, electrical, and mechanical. Our team handles all permit applications, coordinates all required inspections, and ensures that all work is performed by properly licensed tradespeople as required by the Arizona Registrar of Contractors. We are licensed under AZ ROC #339999. Final permits are closed and a copy of all inspection records is provided to you at project completion.
